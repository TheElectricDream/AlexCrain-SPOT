# spot.arm.coriolisMatrix

**File:** `+spot/+arm/coriolisMatrix.m` · **Package:** [[spot.arm|spot-arm]]

<!-- source: +spot/+arm/coriolisMatrix.m -->

## What it is for

The Coriolis and centripetal matrix $C(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}})$ of the Chaser with its arm, so that the velocity-dependent generalised forces are $C\,\dot{\mathbf{q}}$ ([[The Arm|Math-Arm]]). Generated with the Symbolic Math Toolbox in 2017 (the Christoffel symbols of the mass matrix); it was the diagram's *Coriolis Function1* block and is here unchanged.

## How to call it

```matlab
C = spot.arm.coriolisMatrix(armModel, x);   % x = [q; qdot], 12x1
Cv = C * x(7:12);
```

## Reading the generated code

**Line 8.** Unpack the parameters.

**Lines 18–26.** The attitude, the joint angles and their rates (the base translation doesn't appear: a free-floating planar body has no Coriolis force from pure translation).

**Lines 28–154.** The intermediate terms: products of lengths, masses and rates (e.g. `t16 = (θ̇ + q̇₁)·t6`, the rate of the part beyond the shoulder times its first moment), cosines and sines of the cumulative angles, and the rate sums.

**Line 155.** The 6×6 matrix by columns. Its first column (and row) is zero: the base's velocity creates no Coriolis forces. The rest couples the rotations: the terms in `sin(q2)`, `sin(q3)`, `sin(q2+q3)` are the centripetal and Coriolis effects between the links, and the terms with `t15`, `t21`, `t28` (cosines of the cumulative angles) are the forces on the base from the links' rotation.
